Auto-answer: three directions

Same document, same cards, same words, different layout. Each direction opens on step 1. The strip at the top of every screen walks the seven steps, and the branches hang off steps 4, 5 and 6.

Same in all three:
  • The mode is called Auto-answer on the page.
  • Turning it on shows what Claude may do, who can make it act, and what it costs, before it starts.
  • Replies land on the cards exactly as a chat agent's do today.
  • A failed run keeps every comment waiting, and says why in plain words.
  • A failure or the usage limit uses a full amber border, never a stripe down one side.

A. The one quiet line

Argues auto-answer is one more answer to the question the status line already answers, so it adds words there and a menu item, nothing else. A failure borrows today's overdue banner at the top.

Gives up: stopping is two clicks behind a menu, and usage hides in hover text.

Signature: the status line, "Stored · auto-answer working on 2, 35s".

B. A switch beside Hold

Argues the mode is a control over the conversation with the agent, like Hold sending, so it sits beside Hold as a second pill: one click on, one click off, the run count beside it.

Gives up: one more pill in the footer, and a failure shows at the foot rather than the top.

Signature: the Auto-answer switch beside Hold sending.

C. An agent strip under the tabs

Argues an agent nobody is watching deserves a visible seat: a strip under the tabs while it is on, with what it is doing, its run count, and a Stop that is always one click away. Cards say "In this run" or "Next run".

Gives up: vertical space on every screen, and a run looks different from a chat agent.

Signature: the strip, with its always-visible Stop.

Recommended: B, with C's "Next run" note considered for the build. The reasons are in 02_wireframe_lahe_agent_sdk.md.

Every direction has:

  • Steps: off, turning it on, on and idle, a comment lands, a run works the batch, replies on the cards, turned off
  • Branches: usage, waiting its turn, a failed run, a comment it could not answer, the usage limit, stopping mid-run, and the card left after a stop

Controls with a dotted underline are not wired.
