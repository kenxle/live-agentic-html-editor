Summary

A Lahe icon in the Mac menu bar. It shows a number when something is waiting on Ken: an agent asked him a question, or an agent answered comments he has not looked at yet. Clicking it opens a menu with those documents, what is open and who is connected, recent documents, the Library, and Quit. It is an optional Mac extra on top of the cross-platform helper. Agents do not change how they work.

Problem, User, and Context

Ken runs several coding agents at once and reviews their work in Lahe. When an agent asks him a question on a card, or finishes answering his comments, nothing tells him. He finds out only when he happens to open that document. The board shows it happening: on review r03fdeebe1fb6, comments sat unanswered while the agent worked elsewhere, and the page could not tell him.

He also cannot see what is running. Open documents and listening agents pile up across a day. A stray session or a forgotten agent stays invisible until he runs lahe status or opens the Library.

    Silent --> Later{"does Ken open<br/>that document?"}
    Later -->|"by chance, later"| Sees["he sees the answer"]
    Later -->|"no"| Stuck["the agent waits,<br/>or the answer goes unread"]

Ken works at his desk with terminal windows and many browser tabs open, and keeps review tabs open for days. He keeps the Dock hidden. The menu bar is the one place he can see without switching windows.

The user is Ken today, on a Mac. Later, other developers who run coding agents on a Mac. For some of them, seeing how much is open is a satisfying sign of how much they have going on.

Prior work: the crucible, and the stable addresses work, which deferred this icon as its own follow-up (decisions page). Since stable addresses, lahe install starts the helper at login, and the Library has its own address, library.lahe.localhost.

Non-Goals

  • Not a menu bar icon on Linux or Windows. The helper's new summary is plain cross-platform code, so a tray icon there could come later.
  • Not macOS notifications, sounds, or pop-ups. The icon changes; it does not interrupt.
  • Not a way to act on a card from the menu. Answering, dismissing, or reopening still happens on the page.
  • Not a ready-built, signed app download. The icon is built on the user's Mac.
  • Not a Dock icon or a Dock badge.
  • Not a change to how agents work. No new command for an agent, and nothing new in the contract.
  • Not naming agents ("Claude", "Codex"). Lahe does not record an agent's name today (board row LAHE-agent-name-default). The menu names each session by what it is working on.
  • Not a replacement for the Library. The menu is a short list; the Library stays the full one.
  • Not a way to dismiss a card. That is board row LAHE-dismiss-card, and it affects when a question stops counting (R5).

Solution Outline

  1. The helper gets one new answer: a summary of what needs Ken and what is running. It holds the "waiting on you" documents, the documents waiting on agents, the open documents, the connected sessions, and the recent documents. Only Ken's own programs can read it.
  2. The helper notes when Ken has looked at a page. When a page is the focused tab in his browser after an agent's answer on that page arrived, that answer counts as seen.
  3. A small Mac app draws the icon and menu. It asks the helper for the summary every few seconds and shows it. It is Swift with no outside packages.
  4. lahe install offers the icon on a Mac. It builds the app with Apple's compiler, which comes with the Xcode command line tools, and starts it at login next to the helper. When Lahe is updated, the app is rebuilt.

Requirements

The icon

R1. The icon shows how many documents are waiting on Ken, and nothing else. It counts documents, not cards, because each one is one click. With nothing waiting it is the plain Lahe mark. Above 99 it shows "99+". It looks right in a light and a dark menu bar.

R2. The icon never shows a number it is not sure of. It has three looks besides the count: nothing waiting (the plain mark), the helper is not running (the menu offers Start Lahe), and "can't tell" (the helper did not answer, refused, is a different version, or the Mac just woke). "Can't tell" never shows the plain mark or the last number.

R3. The count is quick and right. A new question or answer shows within 10 seconds. An answer clears within 15 seconds of Ken focusing the page it is on. A count that never clears, or counts something already handled, is a bug.

What "waiting on you" means

This is not a new flag. A question card (R5) is the card the rail already shows in the accent, its agent block marked as the thing you need to answer. Today you see it only on a page you have open. The icon counts those same cards across every document. Answered comments on a page you have not looked at (R6) are the one new signal; the rail has no mark for them. The Library's "N waiting" is the other direction: your comments waiting on an agent. In the menu that is R9.

R4. Only replies from after the icon was installed count. On day one the count is zero. Today 45 documents have an old unanswered question; none of them count.

R5. An agent asked Ken a question on a card. The document counts until one of these happens: Ken answers on the card, an agent replies to the card again, the card is deleted, the review is retired or ended, or the document's file is gone. There is no other way to clear a question until card dismissal is built.

R6. An agent answered comments on a page Ken has not looked at since. The document counts until that page is the focused tab in his browser after the answer arrived. A tab open in the background does not count as looking. In a review of several pages, looking at one page clears only that page's answers.

R7. Retired reviews, ended reviews, and documents whose file is gone never count as waiting on Ken. They cannot be acted on from a page.

The menu

R8. Waiting on you comes first. Every waiting document is listed by its name, with a short reason ("1 question", "3 answered"). Clicking one opens it in the browser at its address.

R9. Waiting on agents is shown separately. The number of Ken's comments no agent has answered yet, labeled apart from "waiting on you" so the two are never confused, with its documents listed.

R10. What is open and who is connected. An open document is a review page open in a browser window right now, focused or not. A connected session is one whose agent has a live monitor or ran a lahe command in the last few minutes, the same test the Library uses. Each shows its count and its list. A session is named by its Library name, else the document it is working on, and shows when it was last heard from. Clicking a document opens it. Clicking a session opens its most recent document.

R11. Recent documents. The documents Ken worked on most recently, newest first, as the Library orders them. Clicking one opens it at the address the Library's Open would use. Opening from the menu never asks an agent to pick a document up; a document only an agent can serve opens the Library instead.

R12. Long lists stay short. The open, connected and recent sections show up to 8 rows and say how many more there are. Recent ends with "Open the Library". Waiting on you is never cut short.

R13. Open the Library. One item opens the Library in the browser.

R14. Quit has two items. "Hide the icon" closes the menu bar app only; the helper keeps running. "Stop Lahe until next login" stops the helper. A line under it says plainly that a connected agent will start it again, usually within seconds. Whether Stop should hold instead is a question for Ken.

Installing and keeping it working

R15. The icon is optional and Mac-only. On a Mac, lahe install asks whether to add it; a flag answers without asking. Saying no, or a Linux or Windows computer, changes nothing else about Lahe. Someone already installed adds just the icon by running lahe install again.

R16. A Mac without Apple's command line tools gets a clear message, naming the one command that installs them, and no icon. The rest of lahe install still completes.

R17. The icon starts at login. After Hide, it stays hidden until the next login or until Ken runs one named command to show it. No other command, and nothing an agent runs, brings it back.

R18. Updating Lahe keeps the icon working. When a git pull changes the app, the helper rebuilds and restarts it on its own. If the rebuild fails, the old app keeps running and lahe status says why. An app and a helper that do not match show "can't tell" (R2), never wrong numbers.

R19. lahe uninstall removes the icon and its login item, along with everything else it already removes.

R20. lahe status says whether the icon is installed and running, and names a failed rebuild.

Cost and safety

R21. Light when idle. With nothing changing, the app and the helper's new answer add no more battery and processor cost than one open review tab does today. Measured on a copy of Ken's real state folder, not assumed.

R22. Only Ken's own programs can read the summary. A web page open in the browser cannot read it, from any address, including Lahe's own pages. It names documents and paths, so it gets the same protection as the rest of the helper's private data.

R23. The core stays cross-platform. The helper's summary and the "looked at" record are plain helper code that runs on Mac, Linux and Windows and passes the unit suite there. Only the app itself is Mac code. The tool still runs from a git clone with no packages to install.

How we know it worked

  • On the day it is installed, the icon shows no number.
  • In a week of Ken's normal use, he never clicks the icon and finds nothing waiting, and never finds an agent's question the icon missed. He reports either one on the progress page as a bug.
  • Finding a stray session takes one click instead of lahe status or the Library.

UX Notes

  • The menu reads top to bottom in order of urgency: waiting on you, waiting on agents, open and connected, recent, then the Library and Quit.
  • The icon is a template image, so macOS colors it for a light or dark menu bar and for a highlighted state.
  • Long names are cut short with an ellipsis; the full name and path show on hover.
  • The menu uses the Lahe mark, not a generic symbol, so it is recognizable at a glance.

Analytics / Logging

No new analytics: Lahe is a local tool and sends nothing anywhere. The app writes its own errors (cannot reach the helper, cannot read its permission, rejected address) to a log file in the Lahe state folder. A failed rebuild is logged there too, so lahe status can name it. The helper logs nothing per summary request, because the app asks every few seconds and that would flood the log.

Rollout / Flags

No feature flag. The icon is opt-in at lahe install, which already needs a terminal. Ken already ran lahe install for stable addresses, so he gets the icon by running it again. Nothing changes for anyone who does not opt in. The helper's summary ships to everyone and costs nothing until something asks for it. The "looked at" record starts the first time the new helper runs, which is also the day-one cutoff (R4). This is a minor version bump.

Questions and Decisions

For Ken to decide in review:

When should an open question stop counting? Today 45 documents have an old unanswered agent question, and most are from work Ken has moved on from. The brief keeps what you agreed: a question counts until it is answered or the card goes away (R5), and only questions from after the install count (R4). The risk: questions you choose not to answer pile up, and there is no Dismiss button yet (board row LAHE-dismiss-card). Two other ways:

  • A. Keep R5 as written, and build card dismissal soon after. (Recommended: it keeps a question in front of you until you deal with it.)
  • B. A question stops counting once you have looked at its page, like an answer does. The count clears more easily, but a question you glanced past stops nagging.

Should "Stop Lahe" hold while agents are connected? As designed, the next lahe command an agent runs starts Lahe again, and a listening agent runs one within seconds. So with an agent connected, Stop barely stops anything. The menu line under it will say so plainly. The security reviewer points out that people reach for Stop when something looks wrong.

  • A. Keep it as designed: agents can start Lahe again, and the menu warns. Nothing an agent is doing breaks. (Recommended: you rarely stop Lahe, and a stop that breaks your agents mid-review costs more than it saves.)
  • B. Stop holds until the next login. Agent commands refuse to start it and tell the agent you stopped Lahe. Only Start in the menu, or a command you run, brings it back.

Settled with Ken in the crucible:

  • Which version? The icon that says when something is waiting on him, plus recent documents and what is open and who is connected. The running indicator, Library link, and Quit stay in.
  • A real Lahe icon in the menu bar. A SwiftBar script was ruled out (no Lahe icon), and so was a Dock badge (he keeps the Dock hidden).
  • Native Mac code is fine as long as it is optional and the core stays cross-platform.
  • Built on his Mac with Apple's command line tools is fine. A Mac without them gets a message and no icon.
  • A wrong count is worse than no count. The first version counts only what Lahe knows for certain.
  • Agents do not change how they work. The icon is another way of seeing and reaching what the helper already records.
  • Quit has two items: hide the icon, or stop Lahe until next login.

Chosen by the brief, for Ken to overrule in review:

  • The icon counts documents, not cards (R1). Each one is one click.
  • Looking means the page is the focused tab (R6). Not per card: Lahe cannot know which cards he read, and a per-card rule would need a new gesture on the page.
  • Only replies after the install count (R4). Otherwise the first day shows 45.
  • The icon shows only the "waiting on you" count (R1). Open documents and connected agents stay in the menu.
  • Sessions are named by their Library name or document (R10), because Lahe records no agent name today. When board row LAHE-agent-name-default lands, the menu can show "Claude" or "Codex" too.
  • "Stop Lahe until next login" can be undone early by an agent (R14). An agent's lahe review asks for a helper, and refusing would break that agent. The menu says so.
  • Up to 8 rows per list (R12), tuned on the real menu.

PM review: Menu bar app brief

Reviewed: 01_brief_menu_bar_app.md and its leaf parts/brief/questions.md, against the crucible.

Summary

The brief is faithful to the crucible and mostly the right size. The menu half (what is open and who is connected) is close to ready. The icon half is not: the count it promises can be wrong in the most common case, and on the first day.

Four things to fix before architecture:

  • RF1: "looked at" counts a background tab as looking, so the count would mostly read zero for the very case it exists for.
  • RF2: on the day it is installed, the count would include every old review on the machine.
  • RF3: when the app cannot get an answer, it shows the plain mark, which reads as "nothing waiting". That is a wrong count.
  • RF4: a question Ken does not want to answer has no way to clear.

Scope against the crucible is fine. The small additions (listing the documents behind "waiting on agents", offering to start a stopped helper, clicking a session to open its document) are cheap and fit the two jobs. No settled decision is reopened here.

Findings

Ranked worst first.

RF1. "Looked at" counts a background tab as looking

  • severity high
  • kind defect
  • where R5 (answered since you looked), Solution Outline step 2, questions.md "When an answer counts as seen"
  • what An answer counts as seen when a page of that review is open in the browser after it arrives, and an open background tab meets that rule.
  • why Ken keeps many tabs open, and since stable addresses a review tab stays open for days. When an agent answers into a tab that is already open, the rule is met the moment the answer lands, so the count rises and clears within seconds without Ken seeing anything. The count stays at zero in exactly the case the feature is for. The brief's own evidence (review r5c67aef9754a, where Ken missed an answer) may well have been this case, and the brief does not check it. A smaller hole sits beside it: for a review that covers a folder, opening any page clears answers that arrived on a different page.
  • fix Define "looked at" as: the page that holds the answer was the visible tab, in the window in front, after the answer arrived. Say "the page that holds the answer", not "a page of that review". Add a check that the rule would have caught both reviews named in the Problem section. If the architecture finds Lahe cannot know this for certain, then by the crucible's own premise (a wrong count is worse than none) the answered half of the count should not ship in the first version, and the brief should say that fallback now.

RF2. The first-day count includes every old review

  • severity high
  • kind defect
  • where R4 (an unanswered question), R5 (answered since you looked), R3 (the count is right)
  • what Neither rule says which reviews are in scope, so every agent answer and every question ever recorded on the machine counts.
  • why No "looked at" mark exists for any answer before this ships. The first count Ken sees would include months of old answers and old questions from closed sessions, retired reviews, and files that have since been deleted. A first count of dozens teaches him to ignore the number, which is the failure the crucible names ("ignored within a week").
  • fix Add a requirement that sets the scope. A suggested rule: only answers and questions that arrive after the icon is installed count, and nothing counts from a retired review or a review whose file is gone. Say separately what happens to a question in a closed session (counts, or does not), since the agent that asked it may be gone. Say how a waiting document whose file is gone appears in the menu: listed with "file gone", and a click opens it in the Library rather than a broken page.

RF3. An error must never look like "nothing waiting"

  • severity high
  • kind defect
  • where R1 (the icon shows the count), R2 (the helper is not running)
  • what The plain Lahe mark means "nothing waiting", and the brief gives a different look only to "helper not running".
  • why Many failures are not "not running": the helper refuses the app's request, the app's permission is stale after a helper restart, the helper and app are different versions, the Mac just woke and the last answer is old. In each of those, an app that falls back to the plain mark, or keeps showing an old number, is showing a wrong count, and nothing tells Ken.
  • fix Add a requirement: whenever the app does not have a fresh answer from the helper, the icon shows a distinct "don't know" look, never the plain mark and never the last number. The menu says why in one line. Give "fresh" a number, for example an answer from the last 15 seconds. Keep R2 (not running) as one case of this.

RF4. A question Ken will not answer cannot be cleared

  • severity high
  • kind defect
  • where R4 (an unanswered question): "until he answers or the card moves on"
  • what "The card moves on" is not defined, and dismissing a card is a board item that has not been built.
  • why R3 (the count is right) calls a count that never clears a bug. A question Ken has decided to ignore, or one asked by an agent that has since gone, has no way off the count except answering it. That builds a permanent floor under the number.
  • fix List every event that clears a question, in plain words: Ken replies on the card, Ken rewords it, Ken deletes it, the agent replies on it again, or the review is retired. Then decide what Ken does with a question he will not answer. Either it stays and the brief says so, or the brief names card dismissal (board row for dismissing a card) as something this feature needs first. That second option is Ken's call, since he ranked dismissal below this work.

RF5. What the icon's number counts is not defined

  • severity medium
  • kind defect
  • where R1 (the icon shows the count), R6 (waiting on you comes first)
  • what The brief calls it "a count" but does not say whether it counts documents or individual questions and answers.
  • why The menu row shows "1 question" or "3 answered", so a document with three answers could make the icon read 1 or 3. A tester cannot pass or fail R1 without knowing which, and two builders will pick differently.
  • fix State it. Recommended: the number is how many documents are waiting on Ken, which matches one row per document in the menu. The reasons stay on the rows.

RF6. Architecture decisions are in the brief, including one assigned to the security review

  • severity medium
  • kind defect
  • where Solution Outline step 1 ("with the same proof of permission the lahe command already uses"), steps 3 and 4, UX Notes ("template image"), Analytics, questions.md "Open"
  • what The brief decides how the app proves its permission, while the crucible and the brief's own open question leave that to the architecture and a security review.
  • why The architecture starts with the answer already written down, so the security review is reviewing a choice rather than making one. Step 1's wording, "any program on the computer could read it", also reads as the opposite of R19 (only Ken's own programs can read the summary). The other leaks are smaller but have the same effect: asking "every few seconds", "a few hundred lines of Swift with no outside packages", "Apple's compiler", "template image", and the helper's per-request logging policy are all choices about how to build it.
  • fix Rewrite step 1 as "Only programs Ken runs can read it; how they prove that is decided in the architecture, with a security review." Move the polling interval, the Swift size, the compiler, the template image and the logging policy to the architecture. Keep the product facts they stand for: the app is built on Ken's Mac with no outside packages, it adapts to a light or dark menu bar, and it adds no noise to the helper's log.

RF7. "Show all in the Library" leads to a page that does not show the list

  • severity medium
  • kind defect
  • where UX Notes ("a 'Show all in the Library' item when there are more"), R6 (waiting on you), R8 (what is open and who is connected)
  • what The Library has no "waiting on you" view (its "waiting" is comments waiting on agents) and does not list open review pages.
  • why With many documents, the most urgent list is cut short and the overflow link sends Ken to a page where those documents are not marked. That breaks the first job ("one click gets him there").
  • fix Never cut the "waiting on you" list. Offer "Show all in the Library" only for sections the Library actually shows: recent documents and sessions. For open documents, show the count and say "and N more" without a link. Adding a waiting-on-you view to the Library would be new scope and belongs on the board, not in this brief.

RF8. Clicking a document from the menu can send work to an agent

  • severity medium
  • kind risk
  • where R6, R7, R8 ("opens it at its address") against R9 ("the same way the Library's Open does: same address, same rules")
  • what The brief uses two different rules for a click, and the Library's Open, which R9 adopts, asks an attached agent to pick up a document Lahe cannot open by itself.
  • why A click in a glance menu could quietly queue work for an agent. That sits uneasily with "agents do not change how they work" and will surprise Ken.
  • fix Use one rule for every click in the menu: open the document the way the Library's Open does, but never ask an agent to do anything. A row Lahe cannot open directly opens the Library at that row instead. Also say plainly that a click opens a new tab even if the document is already open in one, so that is a known limit rather than a bug report.

RF9. "One lahe command" brings back a hidden icon, but which one?

  • severity medium
  • kind defect
  • where R14 (the icon starts at login and comes back after Hide)
  • what "Running lahe install again, or one lahe command, brings a hidden icon back" can be read as any command.
  • why Agents run lahe commands all the time. If any of them restores the icon, Hide is undone within seconds and Ken cannot keep it hidden.
  • fix Name the one command Ken runs to bring it back. Say Hide lasts until the next login or that command, and that no agent command brings it back.

RF10. Updating Lahe: no trigger, and only one direction of mismatch is covered

  • severity medium
  • kind defect
  • where R15 (updating Lahe keeps the icon working)
  • what The brief says the app is rebuilt after a git pull "without Ken having to remember" but not what sets it off, and it covers only an app older than the helper.
  • why A pull does not run anything by itself, so the requirement cannot be tested as written. The common case after a pull is the reverse one: a new app talking to a helper still running the old code until it restarts. A rebuild can also fail, for example if Apple's command line tools were removed.
  • fix State the trigger in Ken's terms, for example "the next time the helper starts after an update, it rebuilds and restarts the app". Say both mismatches (older app, older helper) show the "don't know" look from RF3 with a one-line reason, and that a failed rebuild keeps the old app showing that reason and writes the error to the log.

RF11. "Stop Lahe until next login" will not stop it while agents are connected

  • severity medium
  • kind risk
  • where R11 (Quit has two items), questions.md "can be undone early by an agent"
  • what The brief keeps Ken's label but lets any agent's lahe command start the helper again.
  • why A listening agent checks in constantly, so with any agent connected the helper comes back within seconds. Ken clicks a menu item that says it stops Lahe and sees it running again, which reads as a bug.
  • fix Keep the label Ken chose. When agents are connected, show one line under it: "2 agents are connected and will start Lahe again." Add that to R11 so it is tested.

RF12. No way to tell whether it worked

  • severity medium
  • kind defect
  • where Missing section (no success criteria)
  • what The brief has requirements but no success measure, and the crucible's main risk (a wrong count gets ignored) has no check.
  • why Without a stated test of success, nobody decides later whether the count earned its place or should be pulled back to the plain menu.
  • fix Add a short success section. Suggested: over the first two weeks of Ken's use, every wrong count he sees is logged as a bug, and the target is none; Ken no longer learns about an agent's answer by chance. Add a fallback: if the count is wrong more than once in that window and the cause is not fixed, the icon stops showing a number and the menu stays.

RF13. "Who is connected" leaves out working agents, and "open" is not defined

  • severity medium
  • kind defect
  • where R8 (what is open and who is connected)
  • what The menu lists only listening sessions, and "open documents" has no definition.
  • why The Library already treats an agent with no monitor but a recent command as working, and that agent is connected too. Leaving it out hides some of the strays the second job is meant to find. Without a definition of "open", the count cannot be tested.
  • fix List listening and working sessions, marked the way the Library marks them. Define an open document as a review page open in a browser right now, as the helper sees it.

RF14. Two requirements need numbers to be testable

  • severity low
  • kind defect
  • where R3 (the count is right), R18 (light when idle)
  • what "Within a few seconds" has no number, and R18 does not say what state to measure against.
  • why A tester cannot fail "a few seconds". An idle measurement on a test folder with three reviews would pass easily and say nothing about Ken's machine, which holds hundreds.
  • fix Give R3 a number, for example 10 seconds to rise and to clear. Say R18 is measured on a copy of Ken's real state folder.

RF15. Small leftovers

  • severity low
  • kind taste
  • where Rollout ("or a flag that adds only the icon"), UX Notes (staff design eye line), Problem section second paragraph
  • what One undecided choice, one process step, and one claim with no evidence.
  • why The flag is left half-decided, the design review step is plan material, and "open documents and listening agents pile up across a day" is stated as observed pain when the crucible found no evidence for it.
  • fix Decide the flag (yes or no). Move the design review line to the plan. Reword the Problem paragraph to say the second job is Ken's request, not something the board shows.
