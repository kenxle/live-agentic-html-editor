Summary

Section 1

Two workstreams on one feature branch, landing as one PR.

  • core builds the kernel first (the record, the checker, the drain line, the merge rule, the gesture, the tag attribute) and then the browser half on top of it (arming groups, minting a press, the anchor rung, the hold in the outbox, painting state, the rail rows, the chip). One builder, in that order, because every browser row needs the kernel to be real.
  • style-skill builds what agents and readers see, in parallel: the component's CSS and catalog entry, every agent-facing word (the contract paragraph and its two copies, the skill section written with the skill-creator skill), the brand word and the style guide line.

Step 0 wrote the shared names first, so the two builders never edit the same line; the one file both touch, review_format.js, is split by region (core: the field class, projection and export text; style-skill: the contract array).

Step 1: core, the kernel

Section 4

Architecture: The record, The gesture, The script tag, The drain line, Lifecycle

Task 1.1: the record and its checker

Spec: newItem accepts kind: choice with a choice field of {group, answer}. validateItem refuses a choice record missing either, with a value off CHOICE_TOKEN, with any other key inside choice, or carrying before, after, new_blocks or a stamp. isReplayable, isOutstanding and isHandEdit need no change; prove it. The helper's door (log.js) and the projection both run the checker on a choice record. Files: src/shared/record.js, src/service/log.js, src/service/projection.js, their unit tests. Acceptance: V18, V13.

Task 1.2: the format, the drain line and the export text

Spec: PROJECTED_FIELD_CLASS classes choice.group and choice.answer as intent. projectItem leaves before, after_full, html, region_label, subject and the region null for the kind. The drain line matches the architecture's example. The human-readable copy-and-export text prints a choice as its question and its answer. Do not touch the contract array; style-skill owns it. Files: src/shared/review_format.js (not the contract array), src/cli/commands/status.js, their unit tests. Acceptance: V9.

Task 1.3: the gesture, the merge rule and the tag

Spec: PRESS_CHOICE is decided by the gesture function (a click on a button inside an armed .choice, on a document page, in browse, not in pick mode, no block open, window may write, not present; preventDefault true) and has no row in the hint table; adjust the vocabulary and hint-count tests on purpose. merge.js lists choice among browser-owned content fields. protocol.scriptTag takes an opt-in that writes data-lahe-document; page_routes.js passes it on every response for a page the helper serves itself, including a file that already carries this review's tag (added to the served copy, never to disk); add.js and heal.js never pass it. The test harness tag builders (test/browser/support/with_layer.js, test/helpers/) take the same opt-in. Files: src/shared/gestures.js, src/shared/merge.js, src/shared/protocol.js, src/service/page_routes.js, the harness files, their unit tests. Acceptance: V1 (unit half), V8 (unit half), V19.

Step test: npm run gate:unit green; a hand-built events.jsonl with one choice record projects to a review.json whose drain line matches the architecture byte for byte. Commit, then start Step 2 on the same branch.

Step 2: core, the layer

Section 5

Architecture: The anchor engine's choice rung, State on the page, Presses travel together, Key Flows, Failure Modes

Task 2.1: arming and minting

Spec: src/layer/choices.js: on boot and after every repaint, find .choice elements; when the tag carries data-lahe-document, validate each (name, uniqueness, two or more buttons, every value, at most one data-chosen button) and arm it, or set data-lahe-inert and raise the rail chip with the reason, per the refusal flow. Off a document page: inert, no chip. A PRESS_CHOICE gesture mints a choice record (draft then ready in one tick, recordItem with no immediate flush) with context.quote the question and context.heading the nearest heading, and no stamp. A press on a group with a ready record is a reword; after not_handled it reopens the same record; after handled it mints a new one. Files: src/layer/choices.js, src/layer/index.js (boot wiring, the chip). Acceptance: V2, V3, V4, V22.

Task 2.2: the rung, the hold and the paint

Spec: anchor.resolve gains the choice rung keyed on the ref's kind: one match binds, zero or two refuse with their own reason, no text search. splitForFloor holds every event of a choice item until 3 seconds after the page's last press, capped at 10 seconds from the first held press; tab hide and page leave flush at once; Hold's gate stays. replay.js writes nothing for the kind and, after the rung binds, paints data-lahe-pressed on the pressed button and data-lahe-choice-state on the group from the group's latest record by created_at, inside a write epoch. Files: src/layer/anchor.js, src/layer/sync.js, src/layer/replay.js. Acceptance: V5, V6, V7, V10, V20.

Task 2.3: the cards

Spec: tab_active.js draws a choice row: the question as the quote, the answer as a fixed reviewer turn (not editable), the state chip, Mark as done, and the ordinary reply box whose words go into note as a follow-up turn. tab_done.js draws the Done row for the kind with the decided answer. overlay.js gains the kind label ("Choice") and a card summary that shows the answer, not the quote. The cards follow the rail's scheme like every card. Files: src/layer/tab_active.js, src/layer/tab_done.js, src/layer/overlay.js. Acceptance: V11, V23 (rail half).

Step test: npx playwright test test/browser/choice_buttons.spec.js green against the Step 0 fixtures with the worktree's real helper, and the screenshots in progress/ show pressed, open, done and inert groups, and the card light and dark.

Acceptance Criteria

Section 10

Standard (every feature inherits these; keep them verbatim):

  • Full test suite green, not just the new tests.
  • Design and lint gates green (the project's gate command).
  • Every requirement in the brief walked end to end in the browser, on the running app.
  • Nothing punted: no TODOs, no stubbed tests, no "out of scope" that was in scope.
  • Implementation matches brief and architecture; any deviation is deliberate and written down under Changes from plan on the progress page.
  • It looks like a staff designer built it. The whole surface holds up at that level, and includes at least:
    • Clear visual hierarchy and information architecture. You know where to look, and the page's organization makes sense.
    • Consistency with the rest of the app. Buttons look like our other buttons, hover states follow the same convention, primary and secondary carry the same color meaning they carry elsewhere.
    • Honest feedback. Toasts and status messages report what actually happened. Never an optimistic "done" for something still in flight, or something that failed.
    • Micro-animations that mean something. Motion signals state or direction; it isn't decoration.
    • Copy in the brand voice (the project's brand or voice doc).
    • Existing components reused and the style guide followed. Nothing reads as a stock framework default.
  • It reads as one feature, not several agents' work stitched together: consistent components, spacing, and interaction patterns across every surface it touches.

This feature:

  • V1 to V24 green, with M rows evidenced on the progress page.
  • A press never reaches the agent as anything but choice.group and choice.answer at the top level (V9, V18).
  • No page outside the helper's own serving ever arms a group or raises a chip (V1, V22).
  • The lesson queue works with real buttons against the branch's helper before the PR opens (V17).
  • Human has reviewed and approved (single consolidated gate after Plan). Ken said to continue without him; the gate is the progress page he can comment on at any point.

Section 12

Reviewer: review-plan (orchestration and test plan). Read: the plan, the brief, the architecture and its review, CLAUDE.md, the menu bar plan, feature-forge agent-contracts.md and implementation-slices.md, the Step 0 commit d6e08e0b on feat/choice-buttons, and the code each ownership claim names.

Short version. The plan's shape is sound: one PR is the right call, the verification table maps every requirement, and the Step 0 idea (write shared names first) is good. Four things stop it working as written:

  • The layer cannot run a single browser row until most of the kernel has landed, but the plan runs them side by side and treats the kernel as needed only "for the final run".
  • planned: true keeps choices.js out of the bundle, and the layer builder is forbidden to remove it.

  • The lesson-queue preview (the row that proves the lesson queue works with real buttons) uses the bundle-copy trick, which cannot carry the helper-side changes this feature needs.
